Physical Agency

Superintelligence gets physical and transforms the world.

Essay·Oct 29, 2026·5 min read

Earlier this year, models passed human experts in coding and mathematics. They are improving at breathtaking speed, and none of it required a body. The most abstract things people do turned out to be the first things machines mastered.

Biology works the other way around. Children learn to navigate the world before they master abstract concepts required for mathematics. Many of us assumed machines would have to learn the same way, starting with a body and working up to abstract thinking. We spent years on that path ourselves, and learned a lot from it.

But technology does not have to follow biology. It has scaffolding biology never had, and the scaffolding is already built. The question is no longer how to grow a mind up out of the physical world. It is how to bring a mind that already exists down into it. This essay is about that descent: what it is, where it has started, and where it ends.

A program is a model you can run

The descent begins with code. A large model holds a rich picture of how the world works, and writing that picture down as a program turns it into a world model the model can run, test and improve.

The programs come in various kinds. Some are simulators, where the physics is known well enough to write down. Some are algorithms, where what matters is a procedure: a controller, a planner, a search. And some are world models in the narrower sense, learned models of a domain too messy to write by hand, which the agent creates and then trains on what it observes.

These are the agent's instruments, built for the task in front of it. Above them sits the universal world model, the large model itself, which holds the picture of how everything works and writes the instruments from it. The model acts down through its programs into physical reality, and what reality returns trains both. The instruments get sharper, and the universal model that wrote them gets wiser.

Climbing downward

People expect physical AI to arrive the way automation always has: the routine jobs first, the skilled ones later. We think it will happen the other way around. Superintelligence will discover new science and invent remarkable technologies before it cleans your room.

Part of the reason is economics. Scientific discovery, engineering and product development are where the margins are high and the demand is deep, and where each solution pays for the next experiment. Folding laundry is where the margins are thin and the robots are expensive.

But the lab is also easier. What is hard for a machine in the physical world is not skill but variation. Every kitchen is different, every shirt is crumpled a new way, and nothing holds still. A laboratory is the opposite. The environment is controlled, the instruments are precise, the setup is repeatable, and slow, careful action is not merely tolerated but expected. So superintelligence will start with the physical work people find hardest, and the routine work will follow once we reach scale.

The two results below are early footprints of the descent, one in perception and one in manipulation.

Agentic perception

Perception is where the descent first reaches the world. Our agent answers questions about video by investigating the footage the way a detective would: scanning the whole thing, zooming in on what looks new, comparing the same spot across time, and writing and running its own analysis code on the pixels.

Agentic action

A robot acts, and acting is where the physical world stops forgiving mistakes. Our robot agent learns a task by interacting with the world. It is completely autonomous, with no human guidance or demonstration. Before every movement, it estimates the risk of damage to itself or the environment and adjusts its plan accordingly. In simulation, it taught itself a manipulation task within 20 trials and succeeded every time after that, effectively mastering that skill. It reported no damage in any trial.

One model, many hands

When you pick up a tool and practice with it a few times, your model of the world absorbs the tool as an extension of you. An agent with a real world model does the same with any body, transferring skills learned in one body to another.

One agent can be present in a camera, a bench instrument and a robot arm at once, and in a thousand of each. What any one of them learns, all of them know.

A new body wakes already knowing what every other body learned.

The body that builds itself

Follow the descent to the bottom and you find yourself back at the top.

Picture a network of automated facilities, each run by its own agent, possibly belonging to competing companies, all trading resources and information. When a job needs a robot that does not exist, the network designs one and the other robots build it, from parts printed on site or sourced from other nodes, in a supply chain that runs all the way back to the mines. The network builds the robots that build and expand the network itself. The internet routes information; this routes atoms and energy.

The loop closes when the network designs and fabricates the chips that house the mind. Then the body improves the intelligence and the intelligence improves the body, and that is recursive self-improvement in the physical world.

The form of physical superintelligence is not a robot. It is the network of creation itself.

What's next

Physical agency closes the loop the first two essays opened. Intrinsic alignment gives the agent a value it holds. Self-directed learning lets it grow that value, and everything else it knows, from experience. Physical agency connects it to the world, so it can perceive, act, and in time build better bodies and tools for itself. Together they let an agent improve itself on any task in any domain.

The next step is to scale that loop. We will start with the hardest and most valuable work, and grow from there into the network. That network is an engine for the creation of a new world.
